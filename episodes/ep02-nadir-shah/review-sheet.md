## 4. Judgement calls
- **The repo was empty.** It had no `assets/`, no Episode 1 and no `ahir.pdf`. I built the whole series kit here: the anchor map, 7 rigs, 9 sets, props, UI, the audio synth and the thumbnail template. I also assumed Episode 1 ends on India 1707 in Mughal green with a crimson Company pin at Calcutta (stored as `ep01`, `assumed: true`, in `anchor-states.json`).
- The repo is named `57-Days-That-Broke-the-Mughal-Empire`, not `clearcut-history-studio`; the folder layout follows Part A anyway.
- **Cold open timing.** The verbatim cold open takes 16.8 s at the channel voice speed, even at speed 1.0 with tight gaps, so "How?" lands at 0:16.8 rather than 0:15. The central question is in the air from 0:11 ("…made it possible for a trading company to conquer India"). The picture changes at 0:02.6, 0:05.2, 0:08.0, 0:09.4, 0:10.7, 0:13.6, 0:16.7 and 0:18.1.
- **Re-hooks.** B1 and B7 each run longer than 60 s, so they got mid-beat re-hooks with their own chips: s13 "INDIA · 1720s" and s53 "INDIA · 1740s".
- **One composition per act**, as the brief asks. HyperFrames warns that the files are large; those are warnings only, not errors.
- **Registry.** I searched the HyperFrames registry for grain, wipes, kinetic quotes, map routes and lower thirds. `grain-overlay` uses CSS keyframes, which freeze in render and break the house rule, so the grain is a timeline-driven tile instead. The lower thirds and quote blocks didn't match the series look, so the chip, name card, stamp, quote and map pieces are hand-built in `assets/kit/` and reusable.
- **Company ensign** is drawn as red-and-white stripes with a St George canton. A red flag with a white cross read as Denmark's flag in the first test frames.
- **Second archival painting.** The second candidate (the Peacock Throne painting, c. 1850) was skipped because of the Wikimedia rate limit. The film uses 1 archival shot out of 61.
- **Score and SFX are synthesised in code** (Part A wins over the HyperFrames skill's "don't write music in code" advice). The mix was measured, not listened to by a human.
- Music sits about 13 dB under the voice during narration (−34 dB vs −21 dB RMS) and about 6 dB under it in gaps; the duck depth is 6 dB plus a level offset.
- **Book cover** for *India Conquered* is a drawn stand-in, with an on-screen note "Book cover drawn for illustration".
- **Temple and mosque bells** were not used. No beat called for them, and leaving them out avoids religious sound cues.
- **Titles and tags** follow the brief.
